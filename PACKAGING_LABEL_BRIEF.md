# Appa & Amma's Pickles Jar Label Brief

This document defines the first packaging direction for the retail jar label.

## Objective

Create a jar label that feels like it came from a family kitchen, not a packaged-food factory.

The label should communicate:

- warmth
- trust
- small-batch care
- homecoming
- clarity at shelf distance

It should never feel noisy, overly ornamental, or exaggerated.

## Core Label Strategy

The current brand works best when the jar feels remembered rather than advertised.

That means the label should do three things well:

1. establish trust fast
2. make the jar easy to identify by flavor
3. carry a quiet emotional line without becoming sentimental clutter

## Recommended Label Architecture

### Front label

Front label should carry only the essentials:

- brand name
- product name
- short emotional line
- net weight
- small origin line

Recommended front copy structure:

- Brand: Appa & Amma's Pickles
- Flavor: Traditional Mango Pickle
- Descriptor: Avakaya
- Emotional line: A jar from home, wherever home is.
- Origin line: Handmade near Bidar, Karnataka
- Weight: Net Wt. 500 g

### Back label

Back label should do the trust-building work.

Recommended sections:

- short brand story
- ingredients
- how to enjoy
- storage guidance
- batch/MRP/packed-on placeholders
- regulatory block placeholder

Recommended back story copy:

There is a house near Bidar where pickle is still made the slow way.
What began at one family table now travels to others, prepared in small batches with patience, spice, and care.

Recommended support copy:

- Best with: hot rice, curd rice, dal rice, chapati
- Storage: Use a dry spoon. Keep sealed after opening. Refrigerate after opening for best taste.

## Design Direction

### Shape

- primary direction: tall arched front label
- secondary direction: rectangular back label with soft corners

The arch feels more heritage-led and distinctive on a glass jar than a plain rectangle.

### Visual language

- cream base
- dark earth text
- restrained mango-gold framing
- pickle-green accent used sparingly
- fine border work, not heavy decoration
- no loud gradients
- no glossy modern FMCG treatment

### Typography roles

- brand: elegant serif display
- product/flavor: strong readable serif or high-contrast label face
- support copy: clean sans-serif
- optional handwritten accent only for a tiny emotional line, never for critical information

## What To Avoid

- too many slogans on one label
- phrases like best, No.1, premium quality guaranteed
- faux-export visual language
- overcrowded illustrations behind text
- giant product claims that make the jar feel commercial
- mixing too many colors between variants

## Flavor System

Keep the master label structure constant. Change only the flavor band and accent cues.

- Mango: mango orange + mustard gold
- Lemon: warm yellow + olive green
- Bitter gourd: deep leaf green + muted gold

This keeps the range recognizable as one family while making flavors distinct.

## Production Notes

- Front label should remain readable from 2 to 3 feet away.
- Product name must win over decorative elements.
- Story copy should be short enough to read while holding the jar in one hand.
- Leave a defined compliance block rather than weaving legal text into the story area.
- Use matte stock if possible. Gloss will cheapen this brand.

## Recommendation

Start with one hero SKU for development:

- Traditional Mango Pickle (Avakaya)

Once the layout is approved, extend the same system to Lemon and Bitter Gourd.
